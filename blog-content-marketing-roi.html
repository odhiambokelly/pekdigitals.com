Content Marketing ROI: How to Measure and Improve Your Results

PEKDigitals Team •8 October 2026 •4 min read

You have been posting, writing and filming for months. People seem to like it. But is it actually bringing in money? Measuring content marketing return on investment (ROI) sounds complicated, but for a small business it can be done with free tools, a notebook and one honest question to every new customer.

What content marketing ROI really means

ROI compares what you got back with what you put in. The basic formula is:

ROI (%) = (Revenue from content − Cost of content) ÷ Cost of content × 100

Example: in three months you spent KES 30,000 on content (your time, a designer, a few boosted posts), and you can trace KES 120,000 of sales to customers who found you through that content. Your ROI is (120,000 − 30,000) ÷ 30,000 × 100 = 300%.

The hard part is not the maths. It is knowing what your content cost and which sales it brought.

Step 1: Count the real costs

Include everything, even if you did not pay cash:

  • Your time: hours spent planning, writing, filming and posting, multiplied by a fair hourly value.
  • People: freelancers, designers, an agency or staff time.
  • Tools: paid apps, stock images, scheduling software.
  • Promotion: any money spent boosting posts or running ads for content.

Step 2: Track metrics that lead to money

Likes feel good but rarely pay bills. Group your metrics into three levels and focus on the bottom two:

LevelExamplesWhy it matters
AttentionViews, reach, website visitorsShows people are seeing you, but not that they will buy
InterestSaves, shares, link clicks, time on page, newsletter signupsShows content is useful and building trust
ActionWhatsApp enquiries, calls, form submissions, quotes requested, salesThis is where ROI is measured

Step 3: Set up simple tracking

Ask every customer

The most reliable tracking tool in a small business is a simple question: "How did you hear about us?" Record the answer in a spreadsheet with the sale amount. After three months, patterns appear.

Use free analytics

  • Google Analytics shows which blog posts and pages bring visitors, and where those visitors came from.
  • Google Search Console shows which searches lead people to your content.
  • Social media insights (built into Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn) show which posts led to profile visits, link clicks and messages.

Tag your links

When you share a link to your website in a post, a WhatsApp broadcast or an email, add tracking tags (called UTM parameters) so analytics can tell exactly which post sent the visitor. Google's free Campaign URL Builder creates them for you.

Use separate contact routes

A simple trick: use a specific WhatsApp greeting, offer code or landing page for each campaign, such as "Send us 'BLOG10' for 10% off". When customers use it, you know where they came from.

Step 4: Calculate and compare

Every quarter, add up the costs and attributed revenue for each type of content: blog posts, short videos, Facebook posts, newsletters. You will usually find that one or two types bring most of the results. That is valuable information.

Remember that content compounds. A helpful blog post can bring visitors for years after one afternoon of writing, so early ROI often understates its real value.

Step 5: Improve your results

  • Double down on winners. Make more content on the topics and formats that brought enquiries.
  • Update top performers. Refresh your best blog posts with new information and stronger calls to action.
  • Add a clear next step to everything. Every piece should tell the reader what to do: WhatsApp us, get a quote, see pricing.
  • Repurpose. Turn a strong blog post into a video, a carousel and a newsletter.
  • Stop what does not work. If a platform has brought nothing in six months, spend that time elsewhere.
  • Answer buying questions. Content about prices, comparisons and "how to choose" attracts people close to buying. Our website cost guide is an example.

Common measurement mistakes

  • Judging content after two weeks. Give it at least three months.
  • Counting followers instead of enquiries.
  • Forgetting to value your own time as a cost.
  • Not recording where customers came from at the moment they buy.

The simple system

  1. Keep a spreadsheet: date, customer, source, sale amount.
  2. Set up Google Analytics and Search Console.
  3. Tag the links you share.
  4. Review every quarter with the ROI formula.
  5. Do more of what works.
