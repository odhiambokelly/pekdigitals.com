Modern Web Design Trends Every Business Should Know

PEKDigitals Team •6 November 2026 •4 min read

A website trend is only worth following if it helps a visitor do what they came for: understand what you offer, trust you, and get in touch. Here are the design approaches that do that well in 2026, and a few fashionable ones you can safely ignore.

Quick check: open your website on your phone, on mobile data, and time how long it takes to answer three questions: What do you do? Where are you? How do I contact you? If any answer takes more than five seconds, start there.

1. Mobile-first, thumb-friendly layouts

For most Kenyan businesses, the majority of website visitors are on a phone. Designing for the small screen first, then expanding for laptops, produces cleaner pages for everyone.

  • Buttons big enough to tap with a thumb, with space around them.
  • Text at least 16px so nobody has to pinch and zoom.
  • A sticky "Call" or "WhatsApp" button at the bottom of the screen.
  • Menus that are short. Five or six items is plenty.

2. Speed as a design feature

A beautiful site that takes eight seconds to load on 4G loses visitors before they see it. Speed is now part of design, not an afterthought.

  • Use modern image formats (WebP or AVIF) and size images for the screen they are shown on.
  • Avoid auto-playing background videos and heavy animation libraries.
  • Limit fonts to one or two families.

Google's Core Web Vitals measure loading speed, responsiveness and visual stability. Run your site through PageSpeed Insights to see your scores and suggestions.

3. Clear messaging above the fold

The top of your homepage should answer, in plain words, what you do, who it is for and what to do next. "Excellence in innovative solutions" says nothing. "Affordable websites for small businesses in Kisumu, ready in two weeks" says everything.

Pair that headline with one primary button, such as "Get a free quote", rather than five competing ones.

4. Real photography over stock images

Visitors scroll past generic stock photos. Pictures of your actual team, workspace, products and finished projects build recognition and trust. They do not need to be professional, just well lit and in focus.

5. WhatsApp and click-to-call built in

Many customers would rather send a quick WhatsApp message than fill in a form. A click-to-chat link (it looks like https://wa.me/2547XXXXXXXX) opens a conversation with your business number in one tap. Offer it alongside your contact form, not instead of it, so customers can choose.

6. Accessibility that helps everyone

Accessible design means people with visual, hearing or motor impairments can use your site. It also makes it easier for everyone else, for example someone reading in bright sunlight.

  • Strong colour contrast between text and background.
  • Descriptive alt text on images.
  • Forms with clear labels, not just placeholder text.
  • Everything usable with a keyboard.

The international standard is the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG).

7. Visible trust signals

Before someone pays a business they found online, they look for reassurance. Make these easy to find:

  • Your registered business name, physical location and phone number.
  • Recent reviews or testimonials with real names (with permission).
  • Clear prices or starting prices.
  • Terms, privacy and refund policies linked in the footer.
  • Payment options you accept, such as M-Pesa and card.

8. Thoughtful dark sections and bold typography

Large, confident headings and alternating light and dark sections help visitors scan long pages. Used sparingly, they add personality without slowing the site down. This is a trend worth borrowing, as long as text contrast stays high.

Trends you can safely skip

  • Full-screen sliders on the homepage. Most visitors never see slide two.
  • Pop-ups the moment someone arrives. They annoy people and can hurt your search rankings on mobile.
  • Heavy 3D effects and scroll animations that slow down budget phones.
  • Chatbots that cannot answer real questions. A WhatsApp button that reaches a human is usually better.

Putting it together

You do not need to redesign everything at once. Start with mobile speed and a clear headline, then add WhatsApp, real photos and trust signals.
