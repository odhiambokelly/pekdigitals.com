How Much Does a Website Cost in Kenya? A Plain-English Price Guide

PEKDigitals Team •20 November 2026 •4 min read

"How much is a website?" is the question we hear more than any other. The honest answer is "it depends", which is frustrating to hear. So this guide explains exactly what it depends on, gives typical price ranges in Kenya, and lists the costs that often catch people by surprise.

Quick answer: in Kenya, a simple, professional website for a small business typically costs somewhere between about KES 10,000 and KES 70,000 to build, plus roughly KES 4,000–15,000 a year for the domain and hosting. Bigger business sites and online shops cost more. The ranges below explain why.

What actually drives the price

Two websites can look similar and cost very different amounts. The main factors are:

  • Number of pages: a three-page site (Home, Services, Contact) is much quicker than a 30-page one.
  • Custom design vs template: a design made from scratch for your brand takes longer than adapting a ready-made template.
  • Features: online payments, bookings, member logins, multiple languages or integrations with other systems all add work.
  • Content: who writes the text and provides the photos? Copywriting and photography are often extra.
  • Number of products: for online shops, adding and organising 20 products is very different from 2,000.
  • SEO and speed work: a site set up properly for Google and optimised for mobile takes more effort than one that simply "looks okay".
  • Who builds it: a student, a freelancer and an agency charge differently, and offer different levels of reliability and support.

The running costs people forget

A website is not a one-time purchase. Budget for these every year:

Domain name

Your web address, such as yourbusiness.co.ke or yourbusiness.com. A .co.ke domain, managed by KeNIC through accredited registrars, typically costs around KES 1,000–2,000 a year; a .com is usually similar or a little more. Our guide to domains, hosting and business email explains how to choose.

Hosting

The server space where your website lives. Shared hosting for a small business site is often KES 3,000–15,000 a year. Busy online shops need more powerful hosting.

Business email

An address like info@yourbusiness.co.ke. Some hosting plans include basic email; dedicated services cost per user per month.

Maintenance and updates

Security updates, backups, small text changes and fixing anything that breaks. Some businesses pay a monthly care plan; others pay per request.

Payment gateway fees

Online shops pay a small percentage on each M-Pesa or card transaction to the payment provider.

Is a cheap website a bad idea?

Not always. A simple, well-made site at a modest price is often exactly what a new business needs. The problems come from websites that are cheap because corners were cut:

  • The domain is registered in the developer's name, not yours, so you cannot move it later.
  • No access to your own hosting or admin login.
  • Slow, not mobile-friendly or not set up for Google.
  • No backups, so a hack or mistake wipes everything.
  • The developer disappears and nobody can update the site.

Whatever you pay, make sure the domain is registered in your name and you receive all logins when the project is finished.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

  1. What exactly is included, and what costs extra?
  2. Will the domain and hosting be in my name?
  3. Will the site work well on mobile phones and load quickly?
  4. Is basic SEO included (titles, descriptions, sitemap, Google Search Console)?
  5. How many rounds of changes are included?
  6. What are the yearly renewal costs?
  7. Who do I contact if something breaks, and how quickly will they respond?
  8. Can I see two or three websites you have built recently?

How to keep the cost down

  • Start with fewer pages and add more as you grow.
  • Prepare your text, logo and photos before the project starts.
  • Choose a well-built template design instead of fully custom.
  • Be clear about must-have versus nice-to-have features.
  • Pay for the annual hosting plan if it is discounted.

The bottom line

A good website is one of the few marketing investments that keeps working 24 hours a day for years. Spend what fits your stage of business, insist on owning your domain and logins, and make sure it is fast and easy to use on a phone.
