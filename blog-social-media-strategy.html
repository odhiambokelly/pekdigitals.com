How to Build a Winning Social Media Strategy from Scratch

PEKDigitals Team •29 October 2026 •4 min read

Posting every day and hoping something works is not a strategy. It is exhausting, and it rarely brings customers. A real social media strategy is a short, written plan that answers five questions: why, who, where, what and how you will know it is working. Here is how to build one from scratch in an afternoon.

Step 1: Decide what social media is for

Pick one or two business goals. Social media can do many things, but trying to do all of them at once spreads you thin.

  • Get enquiries: people messaging you to ask about prices or bookings.
  • Drive visits: footfall to your shop or traffic to your website.
  • Build awareness: more people in your area knowing you exist.
  • Keep customers coming back: repeat orders and referrals.

Turn the goal into something you can count. "Get 20 WhatsApp enquiries a month from Instagram" is a goal. "Grow our brand" is a wish.

Step 2: Describe your ideal customer

Write a short paragraph about the person you most want to reach. For a bakery in Kisumu it might be: "Working parents aged 28–45 who order cakes for birthdays and office events, mostly on Instagram and WhatsApp, and care about delivery being on time."

This tells you what to post (cake designs, delivery reliability, ordering steps), where to post (Instagram and WhatsApp Status), and when (evenings and weekends, when they plan).

Step 3: Choose two platforms, not six

Being consistently good on two platforms beats being invisible on six. A rough guide:

PlatformBest for
FacebookLocal community, events, older audiences, groups and Marketplace
InstagramVisual products and services: food, fashion, beauty, interiors, events
TikTokReaching new, younger audiences quickly with short, entertaining video
LinkedInBusiness-to-business services, consulting, recruitment, professional credibility
WhatsApp BusinessConverting interest into orders, customer service, repeat customers
YouTubeLonger "how to" content that keeps being found for years

Almost every Kenyan business should treat WhatsApp Business as the place where conversations close, whatever other platform brings people in.

Step 4: Pick three to five content pillars

Content pillars are the recurring themes you post about. They stop the "what do I post today?" panic. For a web design business, the pillars might be:

  1. Show the work: before-and-after website makeovers.
  2. Teach something: quick tips, such as "3 things every homepage needs".
  3. Behind the scenes: the team, the process, a normal working day.
  4. Social proof: client reviews and results.
  5. Offers and calls to action: a clear reason to get in touch now.

A healthy mix is mostly helpful or interesting content, with direct selling roughly one post in five.

Step 5: Choose formats you can sustain

Short vertical video gets the most reach on most platforms right now, but carousels (swipeable image posts), single photos and Stories all have a place. The best format is the one you can produce consistently. Batch your content: film or design a week's worth in one session, then schedule it with the free Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram.

Step 6: Set a realistic posting rhythm

Three good posts a week, every week, beats fourteen posts one week and none the next. A simple calendar might look like this:

  • Monday: tip or how-to (pillar 2)
  • Wednesday: project or product showcase (pillar 1)
  • Friday: review, behind-the-scenes or offer (pillars 3–5)
  • Daily: a quick Story or WhatsApp Status when something real happens

Step 7: Be social, not just a broadcaster

Algorithms reward accounts that start conversations. Reply to every comment and message quickly, ask questions in your captions, and spend ten minutes a day engaging with local businesses and customers. Set up quick replies in WhatsApp Business so you can answer common questions fast without sounding robotic.

Step 8: Measure monthly and adjust

Once a month, look at:

  • Which posts brought the most messages, link clicks or saves (not just likes)?
  • How many enquiries came from each platform? Ask new customers where they found you.
  • What time and format performed best?

Do more of what works and drop what does not. Our article on measuring content marketing ROI shows a simple way to connect posts to sales.

Your one-page strategy template

  • Goal: ______ (a number and a deadline)
  • Ideal customer: ______
  • Platforms: ______ and ______ (+ WhatsApp Business)
  • Content pillars: 1. ___ 2. ___ 3. ___
  • Posting rhythm: ___ posts per week
  • Monthly review date: ______
